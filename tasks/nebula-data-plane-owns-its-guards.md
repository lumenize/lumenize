# Every Resources guard lives in the Resources plane

**Status:** Building since 2026-09-28, ahead of the subdomain build (§ *Transition*); § *Build notes* records each phase as it lands. Pass 2 — phases written against a design intent settled across three review passes. D8, D15, D19 and § *Transition* were re-derived under D22 on 2026-09-27, once [archive/mesh-broadcast-is-core-and-direct.md](archive/mesh-broadcast-is-core-and-direct.md) shipped; § *What the `lmz` handle retires (D22)* holds the result. **Stage 2's third pass ran the same day. D23–D27 record the decisions made from it (2026-09-28), and the body and phases conform to them. The fourth pass (2026-09-28) amended D23 and D25, a targeted fifth pass on that text moved the wipe into the plane, and both were worked into the body and phases the same day.** The 2026-09-25 pass, run on this file as conformed to what [mesh-entry-and-walk-gaps.md](archive/mesh-entry-and-walk-gaps.md) shipped under it, reopened two questions, now D20 and D21. Scoped to the **Galaxy and the Star**, the two hosts that compose the plane. **The Resources leg of the security-legibility work**, after `docs/vision/auth.md`, [ADR-015](../docs/adr/015-passage-and-dominion.md)'s passage/dominion vocabulary, and the Registry routing rewrite that put its guards in one readable table. Judge this as a change to how the access-control model is reasoned about, not as a refactor.

**Objective — answering *"who is allowed to do this to a resource?"* takes reading one bounded region of code.**

**Why: anything the Star and the Galaxy each do for themselves can come to differ, and every difference is a place for complexity, a bug, or a hole to live** (Larry, 2026-09-25). Guards lead because a difference costs most there, but anything the two hosts do separately is in scope for the same reason. The ontology-version check is not a guard, and its two copies had drifted into two different bugs (D20).

**Three goals, in the order they matter:**

1. **The answer stops being split across the host and the plane.** It is in seven places today: `requirePassage` in `onBeforeCall`, the `requireDominionHere` decorators, the identity derivations inside host method bodies, the permission calls inside `Resources`, those inside `Subscriptions`, none in `QuerySubs` (which filters at delivery instead), and the `OrgTree` sites including the dominion short-circuit. Seven places is not an audit, it is an archaeology.
2. **No guard sits where a reader would not think to look.** Those derivations are not plumbing. `clientId` decides whose subscriber row is written and whose row a delete removes, and the code says so: *"`clientId` is supplied by the Handler-1 wrapper from `callChain[0]` (NEVER a param), so a client can only drop its OWN row."* That is a guard, it lives on the host, and it is copied per host — `unsubscribe` matches statement-for-statement across `star.ts` and `galaxy.ts`.
3. **Every name answers "what is this?" without being asked, starting with the plane itself — it is the RESOURCES plane, never the "data plane"** (Larry, 2026-08-23: *"what we will forever more call the resources plane"*). The temporal storage engine is a thing called `Resources` *inside* a thing called `ResourceDataPlane`, and `QuerySubs` opens by calling itself *"a near-clone of `Subscriptions`"*. Names you have to ask about are a model you cannot hold.

⚠️ **Every count in this file is a measurement, not a fact — the phase re-runs each one with an ANCHORED pattern.** An unanchored grep counts the JSDoc prose that discusses a decorator alongside the decorator itself, and both hosts discuss theirs at length; the dominion tally was written wrong twice for exactly that reason before anyone anchored it. The instruments: `grep -rcE '^\s*@mesh\(requireDominionHere' apps/nebula/src/*.ts | awk -F: '{s+=$NF} END{print s}'` and `grep -cE 'callContext\.callChain' apps/nebula/src/star.ts apps/nebula/src/galaxy.ts` (re-measured 2026-09-25: **5**, and **14 + 10**), and `grep -cE '^\s*@mesh\(' <host>` (**21** Galaxy, **20** Star).

The sections below give the guard model these goals imply, the surface it allocates, and the phases that build it.

## Context and current state

**Built already, and what becomes of each part:**

- **`ResourceDataPlane`** — one class, composed by a host with a single `new`, holding five sub-capabilities. **Adapted**: it becomes the plane itself and takes the name, so a host reads `#resources = new Resources(...)`.
- **`Resources`** (683 LOC) — the **temporal storage engine**: Snodgrass snapshot sequences over the host's SQLite, calling `OrgTree.requirePermission` at five sites. **Adapted**: keeps its job, and becomes `Snapshots` (D18) so the plane can take the name.
- **`OrgTree`** (535 LOC) — nodes, edges, grants, resolution, and the `hasDominionOver` short-circuit. **Carried over unchanged**, and it is the model the rest should match: its guards are already in the methods they protect.
- **The per-host data-plane entries** — bare `@mesh()` methods that derive `clientId`/`subscriberBinding`, fail closed, gate the client's pinned ontology version where they take one, then dispatch. **Left behind**: the derivation moves into the door, and D20 moves the version check in with it. Of the Galaxy's 21 decorators and the Star's 20, eight per host are the resource entries and one more is `invite`.
  - **Nothing here is dead, so this is a regrouping and not a deletion.** Every entry still has callers outside its own file; `subscribeQuerySubscribers` has 19. A phase that expects to delete rather than move will find nothing to delete.
- **The host capabilities** — `resetDevData` on the Star, and the three config pairs (D11). They are not Resources operations. The config pairs are **carried over unchanged**; `resetDevData` becomes a call to the plane's `wipe`, the same function a `wipeOnInstall` install runs (D23). `setOntology` is not among them: it carries no decorator at all, and its JSDoc makes that absence the not-remotely-callable boundary, so adding `requireDominionHere` would open a door rather than close one. Only the config setters carry dominion; `getStarConfig` and `getGalaxyConfig` are bare `@mesh()`.
- **The Galaxy's four source entries** — `readSource`, `writeSource`, `buildNow`, `applyOntology` — keep their own `@mesh(requireChatWrite)` rather than collapsing behind the door. They look consumer-less, and that reading is wrong: `LOOP_TOOL_ENTRIES` maps each codegen tool to the entries it may reach, so those decorators **are** the model's authority model, and a test reads the table to assert every named entry sits at the chat floor. A door would hide a mapping a reader currently sees at a glance.
  - **`applyOntology`'s absence from that table is already structural.** `LoopEntry` is a closed union of the three source entries and the table is `as const satisfies`, so naming `applyOntology` in it does not compile (TS2322, reproduced). The obligation is to keep `LoopEntry` closed, not to add a mutation test for a hole the compiler already shuts.
- **`getCurrentOntology` and `getOntologyVersion` are CARRIED OVER UNCHANGED, and both stay BARE.** Their access control is upward passage — the Star's lazy pull of `getCurrentOntology` rides the asking member's own claims, which every member has — so adding a guard because the neighbours have one would break the pull for ordinary members (`calibration.md` §14). A subscription was weighed and rejected: a Star converges on current only when a tab asks for a version it lacks, so a Star nobody is using costs nothing, where a push would fan every Apply out to every Star. ⚠️ **Renaming them belongs to [nebula-ontology-history-file.md](nebula-ontology-history-file.md)**, which reserves the Star-fetch successor — the path both methods serve — as its own decision (§ *Tabled here, decided here: compiled-validator storage*). Its § *The scheme* lists `getOntologyVersion` among the methods that die, and names `getLatestOntologyVersion`, which `getCurrentOntology` has since replaced. They are not Resources operations, and a rename here would land on methods that file deletes or reserves.

- **`invite`, the reapers, and the eviction drains** — host code acting on a *third party's* rows, which is what § *The surface, allocated* exists to place.

### The registries are one kind

**`Subscriptions` (263) · `QuerySubs` (204) · `QuerySubscriberListSubs` (91) · `TreeSubscriptions` (69) merge into one, which keeps the name `Subscriptions` (D18).** All four carry the identical triple `clientId · subscriberBinding · subscribedAt` — the delivery address — and differ on two orthogonal axes: an optional TOPIC column, and an optional AUTHORIZATION identity.

| Registry | PRIMARY KEY | Topic | Authz identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Subscribers` | `(resourceId, clientId)` | `resourceId` | `sub` + dominion bit |
| `QuerySubscribers` | `(queryHash, clientId)` | `queryHash` | `sub` + `profileId` + dominion bit |
| `QuerySubscriberListSubs` | `(queryHash, clientId)` | `queryHash` | none |
| `TreeSubscribers` | `(clientId)` | — | none |

**Every key is `(topic, clientId)` with the topic degenerate where there is none, and two kinds share a topic.** A chat participant is a data subscriber AND a roster watcher of the same query, so both rows carry one `queryHash` — the dual-role shape `subscriber-list.test.ts` pins. That is the merge: one table keyed `(kind, topic, clientId)`, with the tree kind's topic pinned to `''`, since a NULL there either fails the insert or lets one client hold two tree rows. **The kinds differ in AUTHORIZATION, not resource-ness.** A resource or query row gets a permission evaluation on every update, and a roster or tree row does not. The evaluation keys on the row's kind, never on whether a `sub` is present, and the table's `CHECK` constraints make a row that breaks its kind unwritable (D27):

- `kind` is one of `resource`, `query`, `roster` and `tree`, so a mistyped kind cannot slip past the rules below;
- resource and query rows carry `sub`, `profileId` and a dominion bit of 0 or 1;
- only query rows carry `query`, the stored query every re-run reads;
- roster and tree rows carry none of them — so all four columns are declared nullable, the bit as an `INTEGER` with no `DEFAULT`, because porting today's `NOT NULL DEFAULT 0` would make every roster and tree insert fail.

`QuerySubscriberListSubs` opens by calling itself *a near-clone of `TreeSubscriptions`*, which is the same fact stated from the inside.

- **`profileId` is REQUIRED on rows that carry a `sub`, because every token carries it (D27).** Its optionality existed for a pre-rollout token. After the wipe every mint sets it from `Emails.profileId`, which is `NOT NULL` ([ADR-013](../docs/adr/013-identity-profileid-resolution.md) puts it there), so the claim's type becomes required and the `CHECK` backs it. The chat fallback render subscribes to the profile this id names, so an absent id would be a render hole rather than a valid state. It stays absent on the kinds that carry no `sub` — there is no person on those rows to name.
- ⚠️ **The merge silently widens what the drains touch, and the phase owes a decision there.** `clearSubscribers`, `clearQuerySubscribers` and `clearWatchers` are three calls on three tables today, so the Star's ontology install wipes resource and query subscriptions and leaves the org-tree ones alone. Against one table, "clear" has to mean *clear by kind* or an install starts dropping tree subscribers for a reason that has nothing to do with them.
- **`subscribeTree` belongs on every host's `requests`, because the orgTree is the PLANE's, not the host's** (D14). A host does not have an orgTree; its plane builds one, every time. The Galaxy's `onDagChanged` no-op reads like an absent capability and is not one — it is an unwired notification, which D15 deletes by moving the fan-out into the plane that already holds every input it needs: the merged registry has the watchers, `OrgTree` has `getState()`, and the plane broadcasts through its `lmz` handle (D22). ⚠️ **This is the error the `Dag` naming produced**, an hour before D16 was written: *"no org-tree subscribe channel on Galaxy"* plus a hook called `onDagChanged` read as *the Galaxy has no tree*, and a per-host surface was nearly designed on it. `Dag` names the data structure, so the question sounds structural; `OrgTree` names the concept, and "does this plane have an orgTree" answers itself.

### The reload channel is deleted

**The plane is not the mechanism for `.dev` Star reloads, and will not become one.** That is decided elsewhere, not inferred here: [nebula-scope-moves-to-subdomain.md](nebula-scope-moves-to-subdomain.md) § *Decisions* weighed **a subscription to the built digest** — naming this plane, and that it *"already carries enrollment and reaping"* — against a point-to-point nudge, and took the nudge, because a plane subscription *"reloads every teammate watching the galaxy the moment any build lands"* when the signal is wanted for whoever asked and nobody else. Its criteria pin both halves of the successor: only the client that asked for a build has its preview reloaded, and a change Studio makes to the dev Star reaches the dev tab **as a push, with no reload at all**.

**So the channel has no future role, and it has no present one either.** Its original trigger was the ontology-install push to Stars, and the pull replaced it — `nebula-client.ts` says so at the site: on a stale version `Star.read` throws `OntologyStaleError` and the reject path fires `onShouldRefreshUI`, *"relocated from the old push handler"*. `broadcastReload` has no production caller, so a row is written, never fired, and never reaped. ⚠️ Its JSDoc has meanwhile begun inventing a justification — it claims two hosts hold one and that `Galaxy.broadcastReload` fires on build completion, and the Galaxy has neither.

⚠️ **The sweep reaches the scaffold, which is why it is written out rather than left to the phase.** The subscriber is not test-only: `container/app/src/nebula.ts` wires `onReload: () => window.location.reload()` for every dev preview, and that file is frozen verbatim in `scaffold-seed.ts`, so **every generated app ships it**.

- **Source:** `reload-subscriptions.ts`; the Star's field, `subscribeReload`, `broadcastReload` and `onReloadBroadcastResult`; and `resetDevData`'s capture→wipe→restore **with its five-line JSDoc**, which exists only to preserve these rows.
- **Client:** the public `onReload` option, the subscribe-on-connect branch it gates, and `handleReload`.
- **Scaffold:** the `isDevPreview` block in `container/app/src/nebula.ts` **and its comment**, plus the frozen copy in `scaffold-seed.ts`. ⚠️ That comment is wrong today whatever happens here — it tells a user-developer that `onReload` re-syncs their dev preview, and `onShouldRefreshUI` is the only mechanism that fires.
- **Tests:** the baseline helpers `inspectReloadSubscribers`, `callStarInspectReloadSubscribers`, `callStarSubscribeReload` and `broadcastReloadForTest`, and the `reloadCount` capture. `reload-version-contract.test.ts` is **renamed and pruned, not deleted** — next paragraph.

⇒ **`resetDevData` does not survive this as "carried over unchanged."**

⚠️ **`reload-version-contract.test.ts` is mostly the SUCCESSOR's tests, filed under the dead mechanism's name — so it is renamed and pruned, never deleted.** Its header is stale and describes a connect-gate test that no longer exists; its `it()` titles are what it actually tests. It reads two counters for two mechanisms: `previewReadyCount` counts `handlePreviewReady`, the point-to-point build reply the subdomain file decided on, and `reloadCount` counts `handleReload`, the channel being deleted. **T3 — *the build reply goes to the REQUESTER ONLY* — is the one test that tells point-to-point from fan-out**, and reds if a fan-out is ever restored, so it enforces the very decision D17 rests on. Deleting the file would have deleted that enforcement.

## Design intent, constraints, and future state

**The contract, in three tiers.** A Resources operation passes exactly three access checks, each in one place — D20's version rule is not access control, and lives in the plane for the same reason. **The host's `onBeforeCall` requires passage and decides nothing else** — on the Star once [nebula-scope-moves-to-subdomain.md](nebula-scope-moves-to-subdomain.md), which builds after this task (§ *Transition*), deletes its root-admin seed. **The facade a gate returns supplies what the caller may not state** — the client's own id from `callChain[0]` and the binding its updates go to from `callChain.at(-1)` on the request leg, a dead client's id from `callContext.callee` on the response leg. **The plane method below holds permission and ownership**, and is reachable from no wire at all. A forgeable parameter therefore survives on the plane harmlessly, because nothing outside can name the method that takes it.

**What the wire reaches, stated once, because every freeze and audit below counts against it.** A request-leg chain reaches a `@mesh()`-marked member anywhere on the host's prototype chain up to `LumenizeDO`, and whatever is reachable from what that member returns — through `requests`, that includes every public `OrgTree` method behind the `orgTree` getter. Nothing else, since [mesh-entry-and-walk-gaps.md](archive/mesh-entry-and-walk-gaps.md): op 0 of a wire chain must name a marked member, a `svc`-led chain is refused like any other unmarked one, and a reply is data, never a chain. So the response leg runs only continuations a node wrote, never one a client wrote.

⚠️ **The surface is what a member RETURNS, not the member's name — and a returned VALUE carries its own methods.** Past the entry op the walk refuses only the six prototype keys, so a returned Map answers `set` and a returned array answers `push`: a member that hands back state the node decides with hands over the decision. Every member-list freeze in this file compares NAMES, so none of them can see this, and the check belongs with the return value instead (Phase 8). The same goes for a returned OBJECT's own fields. `requests.orgTree` hands out an `OrgTree` instance, so whatever it holds is reachable: every field is `#` today, and a `ctx` or the `lmz` handle held any other way would put arbitrary SQL, or the host's sends, on the wire. So the plane's sub-capabilities get narrow thunks rather than the handle (§ *What the `lmz` handle retires (D22)*), and Phase 8 checks the instance as well as the prototype.

**Fixed ahead of this task, because it was reachable in production** (2026-09-25, in the same spirit as D20's Star half). `OrgTree.getState()` returned the live permission cache — the very object `resolvePermission` reads — through the bare tree gate both hosts carry today. A member could therefore edit the cache and then pass `setPermission`'s own check, making a self-granted root admin durable; on the Galaxy, where the chat node IS the root, that also clears `requireChatWrite` and reaches `writeSource`, `buildNow` and `applyOntology`. It now returns a `structuredClone`, and `org-tree.test.ts` (then `dag-tree.test.ts`) carries the case: the edit runs and changes no verdict. ⚠️ `Object.freeze` is not the fix — a frozen Map still answers `set`. Every other public read already returns a fresh value (`evaluatePermissions` builds its two sets, the traversals build theirs, the rest return a string or a boolean), which is why one method was the whole exposure.

**Two gates, one door.**

```ts
#resources = new Resources(...)   // the plane — the host calls it directly, never RETURNS it

@mesh() get resources()      { return this.#resources.requests }  // request leg
get resourcesResults()       { return this.#resources.results }   // response leg — no @mesh()
```

**The plane owns its two surfaces, so a host does one `new` and nothing else.** `requests` and `results` are named for the LEG, which is the one distinguisher true of every member. A host that assembled them itself could wire them wrong, forget one, or hand the wrong plane to one of them; this way the capability carries its own shape, which is ADR-007's idea applied a level down. ⚠️ **The host still calls the plane directly** for the members on neither surface — `ensureResource`, `findCurrentByField`, `streamProgress`, `wipe` (Studio's Wipe button reaches it through `resetDevData`, D23) and `installedOntology` (the Galaxy's facet probes read it). What it never does is *return* it. The three `clear*` drains stay inside, because the install that calls them is the plane's own (D20).

**`resourcesResults` is not a second door.** `ocan/execute.ts` checks a chain's entry op against the member-level check, so an unmarked getter is refused there exactly as an unmarked method is — and it is refused by reading the property descriptor, so an unmarked getter never runs. `__handleResponse` runs the same path at `requireMeshDecorator: false`, so a fire-back does not consult the mark while `onBeforeCall` still requires passage. A gate with no decorator is therefore response-open and request-closed, and confers that on everything behind it.

**"Response leg" means two different paths, and only one of them runs `onBeforeCall`.** Both are request-closed, so the security conclusion holds for each; what differs is how a `results` member is reached.

- **A reaper runs LOCALLY on the host that broadcast.** `lmz.broadcast` makes a 4-arg call per target, and a Gateway answers inside its own ack rather than early-acking, so `lmz-api.ts` runs the handler in place: `executeFilledChain(filled, nodeInstance, { requireMeshDecorator: false })`. No hop, no `__handleResponse`, so **tier 1 of the contract does not apply on this path at all** — nothing arrives from outside to be admitted.
  - ⚠️ **`callContext.callee` names the failed TARGET only where the handler runs on the broadcaster's own dispatch** — a Gateway target, or a refusal at admission. A DO or Worker target that acks and then throws fires the error back, and there `callee` is the broadcaster itself. Every fan-out in this plane addresses Gateways, so the reapers read the target; a future fan-out to a DO would not, and would reap nothing.
  - ⚠️ **The `result` it receives can be the subscriber's own reply**, relayed by the Gateway. So `result` decides only WHETHER to reap. WHO is `callContext.callee`, which the framework stamps from the address the push used, so a forged error reaps only its forger.
- **`onInviteResult` genuinely arrives through `__handleResponse`**, as a fire-back from the facade, a different node. That path runs `executeEnvelope` at `requireMeshDecorator: false`, and `onBeforeCall` still requires passage.
- **There is no third path.** [archive/mesh-broadcast-is-core-and-direct.md](archive/mesh-broadcast-is-core-and-direct.md) deleted the recursive tier on 2026-09-27, so every fan-out is a direct loop at any N and no forwarded result can arrive as a request. The two paths above are the whole of it.

**The two gates are told apart by the LEG.** Not by where the caller id comes from: `onInviteResult` takes an `InviteSummary` rather than a dead client's id, and `read` needs no caller id at all. The leg holds for every member; identity holds for most.

⚠️ **Two absences carry the design, and both are invisible at the site that depends on them.** Adding `@mesh()` to `resourcesResults` — the tidying reflex, since every other gate has one — opens every continuation target to any logged-in browser in one edit. The one that matters is `onOntologyPulled`. It checks no permission, hands the validator bundle in its argument to the Worker Loader, and runs the install's wipe (D23), so a caller could load a validator of their own and empty a `.dev` Star. `onInviteResult` is the usual example, and it is not the reason: a forged call runs under the forger's own claims, and `setPermission` re-checks `admin` at the node. Spelling a surface's plane reference `private` rather than `#` puts every plane method back on the wire behind the door (D13). **Each owes an assertion that can see an absence**, and `getMeshGuard` cannot: `mesh-decorator.ts` sets `MESH_GUARD` only when a guard argument is passed, so it reads falsy for a bare `@mesh()` and an undecorated member alike. Read the mark off the DESCRIPTOR rather than off the prototype — reading `Galaxy.prototype.resourcesResults` INVOKES the getter with the prototype as `this`, where the `#resources` brand check throws. ⚠️ **The entry rule reads `descriptor.get ?? descriptor.value`** (`markedFunctionOf`), and a check that reads `.get` alone is false in two BROKEN states as well as the right one: the gate was renamed away, or the gate became a marked METHOD. So read the same `get ?? value`, and pair it with a positive control — the descriptor exists, its `get` is a function, and its `value` is undefined. Then assert each surface exposes no member beyond its row in the table.

**Seen from the client, this collapses many ways into one.** Today `nebula-client.ts` reaches the host through a distinct continuation per entry — `ctn<Star>().transaction(...)`, `.subscribe(...)`, `.subscribeQuery(...)`, and the rest — plus the chained `orgTree()` gate. Afterwards every one is `ctn<Host>().resources.<op>(...)`: one way to reach the plane from every caller, and a new client method cannot invent a second. That spelling needs a mesh fix first. `ContinuationMethods` maps every non-function member to `never`, so a chain through a getter does not compile, and mesh's own getter-gate test casts around it with `(admin.ctn() as any).settings`. Phase 6a teaches the type to follow a getter. `OrgTree` is reached as `resources.orgTree.setPermission(...)` — **no parens on `orgTree`, it is a getter** — and `ocan/execute.ts` refuses a chained `apply` on it as not a function. The sweep is every call through today's tree gate: `nebula-client.ts`'s `orgTree` mutators, the baseline test-app's tree wrappers, and the `mesh-entry-reach` scenario, which walks past the gate on purpose. Larry: *"If we have two, why not three? why not 10?"* A second door is not a smaller version of the mess; it is the first instance of it.

**The surface, allocated.** A gate is what makes `public` on the plane mean CALLABLE FROM THE WIRE. Nothing returns the plane today — the only gate returns the tree — so `public` there costs nothing and no method was ever chosen for a wire audience. Neither gate returns the plane; each returns a surface whose member list is the whole of what it exposes.

|---|---|

**The `do*` prefix goes with the PARAMETERS, not with the method.** The plane's ops keep their `clientId` argument; the door derives it and passes it down. That is the whole reason the plane can hold a forgeable-looking parameter safely — and it is the tension the two surfaces dissolve rather than pay. `removeSubscriber(resourceId, clientId)` has two callers of opposite provenance: `unsubscribe`, where a client drops its OWN row and the id is derived, and the reaper, where the id belongs to a client already gone. Self-guarding serves neither — derive, and the reaper cannot name the dead; accept, and the parameter is forgeable. Keeping the raw op on an unreachable plane serves both.

**Why not the two cheaper shapes.** Self-guarding the members on neither surface fails on most of them: `ensureResource`'s server-composed `actor` is the point of the method, `findCurrentByField` takes no node argument to check, and the three `clear*` take no arguments at all, so there is nothing to derive and a wire caller would drop every subscriber on the host. Pulling their callers inside fails on the layer map — the Galaxy's agent-message commit is host business logic, not a resource capability.

**`streamProgress` sends a subscription update on the request leg, and D19 moves it into the plane's host-only set.** It is `protected` on `galaxy.ts` today, building the chat query inline and reaching `targetsForQuery` through the Galaxy's own `queryTargets` seam before `this.lmz.broadcast`. It takes no `result`, and its `progress` and `replyTo` are caller-supplied, so behind a response-open gate any passage-holder could inject arbitrary text into a shared chat attributed to someone else's live turn — `replyTo`'s JSDoc makes that attribution load-bearing against a hang. That is why it lands with the host-only members and never on `results`. In the plane it sends through the `lmz` handle like every other update (D22, D24).

**It moves with query/resource parameter names, so the chat vocabulary stays on the Galaxy.** Today's body builds `{ queryType: 'parentChild', typeName: 'Message', field: 'chat', value: chatId }` itself. Moved verbatim, that puts the chat ontology into the plane every host composes — the layering § *Why not the two cheaper shapes* rejects. The plane's `streamProgress` takes the query; the Galaxy's codegen loop builds it.

**`targetsForQuery` becomes `#private`, and its tests re-route through a real turn.** Once `streamProgress` is inside the plane, it is the only caller. The tests in `child3-targets.test.ts` and `child3-stream.test.ts` reach it today through test-subclass helpers over the `queryTargets` seam (`inspectQueryTargets`, `streamChunkForTest`), and D19 deletes that seam. `GalaxyTest.chatScriptedForTest` already drives `runTriggeredTurn`, which calls `streamProgress` from its heartbeat and from each step, so the tests route through that. `child3-targets` is re-pointed rather than retired: it is the test that isolates the dominion check from the grant check. ⚠️ `child3-stream`'s header says codegen is wrangler-dev-only; the scripted turn made that stale.

⚠️ **Two `invite`s on the client is deliberate — do not "fix" it.** `client.invite(targetScope, …)` reaches the Registry facade and can confer `scopeAdmin`; `client.resources.invite(nodeId, …)` reaches the plane and never requests it. On a Galaxy the facade's `.dev` co-mint still gives the invitee the inviter's dominion over the `.dev` Star, deliberately, and `four-party-chat` asserts it. Both follow `auth.md` § *Grants in both planes*, and they are told apart by the first argument's kind and the `resources.` prefix. The subdomain sibling's standing instruction to name `Galaxy.invite` explicitly means the plane one.

**`subscribeTree`'s own authorization reasoning needs a home, and it is stated for passage, not membership (D21).** Its JSDoc argues there is *intentionally no node-level read check* — a member may watch the tree because ADR-008 makes it visible to every member — and today that sits beside `@mesh() orgTree()`, which D1 deletes. It belongs at the merged registry's tree kind, next to the nullable `sub` recording the same fact, and the roster kind carries the same reason. That reason has to cover a tenant: under D14 anyone with upward passage into a Galaxy subscribes too, and ADR-008 speaks only within a Star. So it is visibility ≠ capability carried up the hierarchy — every action is checked where it is taken, so seeing the tree and who is watching buys a caller nothing to act on — which is what ADR-015's *Deliberately open* consequence asks to see written down.

**Load-bearing claims, stated so review can falsify them:**

- **The reapers carry no host-specific work once Phase 3 lands.** Four on each host from Phase 3, and every body is the same few lines with one word changed: test the result for `ClientDisconnectedError`, read the victim off `callContext.callee`, call the matching remover. ⚠️ **`Star.onTreeBroadcastResult` reads `#treeSubscriptions` until Phase 3** moves that registry into the plane, so "no host state" is true only from Phase 3 on. The plane builds their continuations with a private `#ctn<ResourcesHost>()` — typed as `Continuation<ResourcesHost>` over `newContinuation()`, the shape of `profile.ts`'s `ctn()` — where `ResourcesHost` is the structural type every host implements, so a misnamed reaper or gate fails to compile rather than failing a reap into a log line. Phase 3 declares it over the hosts' reapers and `onInviteResult`; Phase 7 moves the reapers onto `results` with their names intact and reshapes it, so a continuation reads `this.#ctn<ResourcesHost>().resourcesResults.onQueryBroadcastResult(queryHash)`.
  - ⚠️ **The Galaxy has no tree reaper today, and D14 is what gives it one.** Phase 3 adds `onTreeBroadcastResult` to the Galaxy, unmarked, so both hosts satisfy `ResourcesHost` and a dead Galaxy tree subscriber is reaped by construction — but only if a criterion asks, which Phase 6 does once the Galaxy has `subscribeTree`.
- **Moving them closes no hole, because none is left to close.** A reaper used to take its victim off the delivered Error, so a caller-supplied `ClientDisconnectedError` could name somebody else's row. [mesh-entry-and-walk-gaps.md](archive/mesh-entry-and-walk-gaps.md) took the victim from `callContext.callee` instead: a forged reply now reaps only its forger, and a direct request-leg call names the host itself, which matches no row. What the move buys is D1's single door. And no path to a reaper consults the mark — neither the local run nor the fire-back door — so an undecorated `results` member is reached on each.
- **No data-plane entry carries `requireDominionHere`**, so deleting the entries weakens nothing.
- **The plane can nearly see what it needs.** It derives the call context from its `lmz` handle and threads a narrow thunk into four of the five sub-capabilities; `QuerySubscriberListSubs` takes only `ctx`, so the merged self-guarding registry has to add the thread. That is real work, not a fact already true.
- **The client's public surface keeps its name.** `client.resources.transaction(...)` is the app author's vocabulary and its audience is Studio's codegen model; every rename here is internal.

### The orgTree rename (D16)

**`DagTree` becomes `OrgTree`, and this REMOVES a word rather than adding one.** `orgTree` is already the published name — `client.orgTree.*` and `handleOrgTreeUpdate` are what a user-developer reads — while the internals say `DagTree` on every line Phase 1's first instrument counts. Two words for one concept is what `naming-judgment` says to spend down, and `Dag` is the wrong one to keep: it names the underlying data structure, where `orgTree` names what the structure is FOR, which is organizing a permission model. ⚠️ **This paragraph, D16's row and the FROM list below are the places the old name must survive — exclude them from any sweep.** ⚠️ **`calibration.md` §11 is excluded too, and that is not tidiness.** Phase 8 rewrites its worked example wholesale, and it quotes a dated call — `ctn<Star>().dagTree().setPermission(...)` — so a rename there turns a real quotation into a call that never existed, and Phase 8's instruction, which names the pre-sweep strings, then finds nothing and passes having done nothing.

### The two names (D18)

**The storage engine becomes `Snapshots`.** It manages the `Snapshots` table, its only SQL surface, and ADR-004 calls its rows snapshot sequences. The word appears nowhere as a class or type today, so `class Snapshots` beside the existing `Snapshot` type is the obvious pairing and adds no vocabulary.

**The merged registry becomes `Subscriptions`, class and table alike.** It answers *what is this?* on sight: one class holding subscription rows of several kinds. Today's four tables are `Subscribers`, `QuerySubscribers`, `QuerySubscriberListSubs` and `TreeSubscribers`, so `Subscriptions` reuses no surviving TABLE name — which is where a survivor reading would actually mislead. It reuses a deleted class name, and that reads as the union keeping the general word rather than one sibling outliving three.

**Rejected, and why, so nobody re-proposes them:**

- **`Subscribers`** for the registry — it is one of the four table names, so the survivor problem is real rather than notional.
- **`Enrollments`** — tempting, because [nebula-scope-moves-to-subdomain.md](nebula-scope-moves-to-subdomain.md) says the plane *"already carries enrollment and reaping"*. But `enroll` is this repo's MEMBERSHIP word — *"enrolled through the REAL invite"*, *"enrolled in the `.dev` workspace Star"*, *"rendering the offer enrolled the invitee"* — and reusing it for subscriptions is the collision goal 3 exists to prevent.
- **`SubscriberRegistry`** — `Registry` is `NebulaAuthRegistry`'s word and a top-level concept here.
- **`Watchers`** — already means the query-subscriber-list kind specifically (`clearWatchers`, `removeQuerySubscriberListWatcher`).
- **`TemporalStore` / `SnapshotStore`** for the engine — new vocabulary for a table that has a name. **`History`** — ADR-004 calls history the substrate, not the engine.

⇒ **The subsystem lands at four names, every one already in the reader's head:** `Resources` the plane, `Snapshots` the storage engine, `OrgTree` the permission tree (D16), `Subscriptions` the merged registry.

### One version rule (D20)

**A host serves one ontology version, its source's current one, and the plane enforces that the same way on every host.**

1. A client pinned to the installed version proceeds.
2. Otherwise the plane asks its source for the current row, and installs it if it differs from what is installed. The pinned client proceeds if it named that row — at once when the source answers at once, or on its retry after `installing` when the source answers by fire-back.
3. Any other pinned version is answered `OntologyStaleError`, and the client refreshes onto the current bundle.

**An op that names no version takes the same path when nothing is installed** (D26). The plane asks its source, and a source that answers later means the op is answered `installing`. So `invite`'s first-touch request, the Galaxy's self-seed and an unpinned `subscribeQuery` on an empty Star are all this one install. The client retries `installing` the same bounded way on every op, every kind of subscribe included, and a resource subscription no longer mistakes `installing` for a stale bundle.

**Each host keeps only its source (D3).** The source returns the current row, or nothing when it has asked and the row will arrive at `results.onOntologyPulled`. The Galaxy's chat ontology ships in the Worker bundle (`CHAT_ONTOLOGY_SEED_ROW`), so its source answers at once. The Star's app ontology lives in its Galaxy's registry, so its source is a `getCurrentOntology()` call answered by fire-back, and `onOntologyPulled`, where that fire-back lands, becomes a `results` member (Phase 7). The check, the install, the clear-by-kind drains, and the three ways an operation answers a mismatch — `transaction` returns it, `read` throws it, `subscribe` pushes it — are one piece of code in the plane.

- **What differed between the two installs becomes data, or goes.** The plane loads a facet with `env.LOADER`, read through a module-scope `import { env } from 'cloudflare:workers'` as `entrypoint.ts` already does, so the constructor stays at four arguments. The source supplies the bundle-id prefix: a Star's keys on its parent Galaxy so sibling Stars share one bundle, and the chat ontology's adds `/chat/`. One KV keyspace serves both installs, because the chat keyspace's stated reason — keeping chat rows out of an app registry Stars read — expired when that registry moved from KV to files. The cache fields move into the plane.
- **A `wipeOnInstall` row wipes only when it replaces an installed version, and only on a `.dev` Star (D23).**
  - **A first install never wipes.** A tenant Star's first `invite` after a breaking Apply installs normally, and an org tree built before a Star's first install survives it.
  - **A non-`.dev` host never wipes.** `applyOntology` decides the bit against dominion over the `.dev` Star, but the bit rides the row every Star under the Galaxy pulls, so this refusal is what keeps a sandbox decision off a tenant's data; a Galaxy's breaking chat change is refused the same way. `wipe` refuses first, by throwing, before it records or changes anything. The Star's install catches the throw in its fire-back handler and records nothing, and a Galaxy's install, which runs inside the op, turns the throw into the stale answer. After pre-alpha such a host migrates instead. Until then a client pinned to the refused row ends at the stale signal: on a Star after its `installing` retries run out, on a Galaxy at once.
  - **On a `.dev` Star the plane erases what it owns and rebuilds itself.** The install captures the subscribers it must tell, then `wipe` drops every table and storage key the plane owns — each part's tables and their migration markers, and the installed ontology — and rebuilds its parts with the build function its constructor runs, so a grant the wipe erased stops authorizing at once. It is all synchronous, so no other event runs in between. Mesh's identity is never touched, which is what lets the same call send afterwards; today's `deleteAll()` erases it, which is why today's install throws on `this.galaxyId` straight after its wipe. The host's own state outside the plane — today only the Star's `config` entry — survives.
  - **The install then finishes on fresh parts.** It records the row and tells the captured subscribers through `lmz.broadcast`. Every wipe bumps the plane's generation number, and a transaction takes the number when it starts and refuses to commit if it changed, so one paused at the validator's `await` across a wipe answers stale rather than writing into the fresh tables. Phase 6 witnesses this on both wipe paths.
  - **Studio's Wipe button runs the same `wipe` through `resetDevData`.** It records no row and tells every subscriber, and the next op's install is then a first install, so nothing wipes twice.
  - **`wipe` writes one ADR-016 record**, after the `.dev` check, of the claims in context, labelled with its cause: whoever pressed Wipe, or the member whose op triggered the install, with the version. `applyOntology` already records who decided that version's wipe, so the two join on the version.
- **Going back to an earlier version is [nebula-ontology-history-file.md](nebula-ontology-history-file.md)'s to shape.** Today `#appliedHead` reads the source file first, so a chat-floor revert of `src/ontology.d.ts` to an applied version makes that row current with no Apply. Its `wipeOnInstall` then wipes `.dev` with nobody's dominion checked. That file's append-only history makes going back a new entry, and its file carries the requirement.

**Two copies of this check drifted into two bugs — this task's case in one example.** Both hosts stored one installed version and compared the pinned version against it. They differed only on a mismatch, and each side's answer was wrong:
- **The Star installed whatever version a client pinned, an older one included.** A tab still on v1 after v2 was applied — a collaborator's preview, say, since the build reply reloads only the requester's — made the Star pull and install v1. `#installState` deleted v2's row and dropped every subscriber, while `setOntology` left v2 as the last entry of the history it reads the installed version from. While warm, v2 clients were validated against v1; after eviction every operation threw *index/row drift*; a `wipeOnInstall` v2 re-wiped the `.dev` Star on each flip back. ⇒ An install must record its row as THE installed one in the same write — the drift was a row installed while the record still named another.

## The push path — what it stores, and what it rechecks

**Reading a permission costs no storage.** `OrgTree` keeps the tree as a lazily-built state cache with an adjacency index over it, invalidated on every mutation, and `resolvePermission` walks that index in memory. One `evaluatePermissions` call is a map walk, not a read. So the cost objection against checking at push time is weaker than it looks, and the design question is which shape stores the least, not which runs least often.

**The stored `dominionOverHostAtSubscribe` bit exists because the push path has no token.** A Galaxy or Universe admin holds no grant in the host's tree — they reach it by dominion, which is a fact about their claims. Nobody is calling when a push goes out, so a bare grant evaluation would drop exactly those people. The bit is that remembered claim, and something like it is unavoidable while pushes are authorized at all.

⚠️ **What is wrong is not that the bit is stored, it is what the recheck's own comment implies.** `evaluatePermissions` takes the bit as a short-circuit, so for a dominion-holding subscriber the tree is never consulted and the verdict is the one frozen at subscribe. The recheck is live for grant holders and a replay for everyone else, while the comment presents it as uniformly live. By ADR-010's replication rule the bit is a copy of a mutable fact and owes a stated convergence mechanism; it has one — the client re-subscribes on reconnect, re-deriving the bit from the fresh token — but that is written only in `NebulaClient`'s resubscribe walk, on the other side of the wire from the column it converges. **State both at the column.**

**Keep the per-push recheck. It stores the least of the available shapes.** For grants it evaluates live and remembers nothing, so there is no second verdict to go stale. For queries it is not a freshness check at all — it computes the allowed and denied sets per subscriber, which is what lets a denied subscriber be told *which* nodes they lost rather than silently dropped ([ADR-008](../docs/adr/008-full-org-tree-visibility.md)) — so it cannot move regardless. And the obvious alternative is worse: skipping the per-commit evaluation means materializing an allowed flag per row for the commit path to read, a second stored verdict beside the bit, and [ADR-013](../docs/adr/013-identity-profileid-resolution.md) calls a stored authorization verdict a security bug.

**There is no revocation window on either path.** A revoked grant is caught by the very next update, because every update runs from a commit and evaluates live. Nothing is delivered in between, so nothing escapes. The server re-runs nothing on a permission change (D25). The re-run of every live query it used to fire did two jobs, and neither closed a hole: it disclosed a loss promptly, and it delivered a gain without waiting for a write, the case `child2-query-permission.test.ts` was written for. A loss now waits for the next update to what the subscriber watches. A gain reaches a client watching the org tree at the next tree change, because that client re-subscribes whatever its last update denied, and reaches any other client at the next update. Every client `createNebulaClient` builds watches the tree, so the cost is one re-subscribe per denied subscription per tab at each tree change, and nothing for a tab with no denial.

**No subscribe is refused for permissions, and the merged registry keeps no register-time check (D25).** A subscriber sees what it can read and is told which nodes it can't. A refusal naming the missing nodes would also satisfy ADR-008, but it would cost the subscriber everything it can read. A resource subscription follows the same rule in the query's shape: one whose subscriber can't read the resource registers, its `.snapshot` resolves `null`, and `deniedNodes` names the node, where today `Subscriptions.subscribe` refuses it and `#broadcast` later skips it without a word. Only a permission denial becomes a denied registration; a missing resource, a type mismatch and an unauthenticated caller still reject and write no row. So a subscribe is not an exception to *every method guards itself* — its guard is authentication plus the delivery-time evaluation, and **that is what the method must say.** Recording it as *"always succeeds — no permission check"* is what made it read as a hole for two reviews running.

## Retro (2026-09-28)

**What we learned.** The build's one wrong idea was a reason, and the task file had already named it. § *Design intent* says `onInviteResult` "is the usual example, and it is not the reason" for leaving `resourcesResults` unmarked. The build wrote it as the reason anyway, in `resources.ts`, `star.ts`, `galaxy.ts` and a test header, and Phase 8 copied it into `mesh.md`, where every later session would read it. Nothing could red it: the conclusion was right, and only the sentence after "because" was false (`calibration.md` §7). The panel caught it by reading the task file against the code.

**What we struggled with.**
- **The wipe's drop order.** Dropping `Nodes` under a `Snapshots` row fails its foreign key, so the first run reddened all four reset tests.
- **An empty Star refuses a resource subscribe.** The resource kind's `installing` retries therefore needed an upgrade fixture rather than an empty host.
- **A red mutation run hung.** A failing limb left its clients open, and the vitest process never exited until each limb disposed in `finally`.

**Tests that failed unexpectedly.**
- **`first-app-built` limb 6, Phases 3 to 7.** The built app's refresh at the Galaxy's scope 401s, because Studio's cookie is path-scoped to the Universe, and the scaffold then navigates to `/login`. The subdomain build retires that navigation.
- **`first-app-built` limb 5, once in Phase 7.** Workers AI answered REST 400 mid-turn, so nothing was built; the rerun built the app.
- **`scope-isolation.test.ts`'s alarm wait, once each in Phases 1 and 4.** Its own comment says CPU starvation stretches that 15 s wait. Alone it passed 38 of 38 three times after Phase 1, and 5 of 6 runs after Phase 4.
- **The embed check at the closing run.** The Phase 4 fix edited `api-reference.md`, which the Worker embeds, and `gen-platform --check` refused the stale embed. The rule that the closing run follows the last edit is what caught it.

**Impact on follow-on work.**
- **`OntologyState` in `galaxy.ts` was exported and read by nothing.** It was the return type of `getLatestOntologyVersion`, which `6173bb9` retired; removed with its two re-exports on 2026-09-29.
- **`Galaxy.getOntologyVersion` has no caller outside tests.** Its decorator states what it serves; whether anything needs it is open.
- **The Star's root-admin seed latch survives a wipe while its grant does not.** A `.dev` admin can reach it today, at no cost in capability, and the subdomain build deletes the seed. The build note first called it unreachable; Larry's question on 2026-09-29 is what had it checked.
- **`client.resources.invite` and `client.invite` are both absent from `api-reference.md`.** That page has never documented the facade invite either.

**Process change — none; one was proposed and Larry declined it on 2026-09-29.** `/build-task` § *Implement*'s paragraph on an invented reason covers a kept parameter. It should also cover a reason given for leaving something open. Proposed sentence, after *"An invented justification is worse than none"*: *"The same holds for a reason given for leaving something unguarded or unmarked: grep the task file for it first, since a design that names a tempting wrong reason is telling you which sentence you will write, and trace the forged call to the first check it meets. On 2026-09-28 a task file said `onInviteResult` 'is not the reason' for an unmarked getter, and the build wrote it as the reason in five files, one of them `mesh.md`."*
