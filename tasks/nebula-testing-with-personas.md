## Context

One of Lumenize Nebula's main selling points is that every app built automatically gets a robust fine-grained access control model using our DAG orgTree model for Resources.

Right now, the preview in Nebula Studio is a single iframe. The user-dev is expected to log in as founder, invite other users with their own dummy email addresses, accept those invites, and then log out as founder and in as each of those other users one at a time to test their fine-grained access control and see how the UI changes based upon those permissions. That does not align with Lumenize's de✨light✨ful DX core value.

Further, we want to guide each user-developer to good software development practices and one of those practices includes utilizing a cast of named personas to think about the experience of their users. We already have some support in the platform standing guidance for encouraging this but we have more planned including testing. That guidance is `apps/nebula/platform/`: `AGENTS.md` names the cast file, and `apps/nebula/platform/skills/define-the-cast/SKILL.md` is the skill that elicits it. The files the model writes into the user-developer's Workspace repo are `docs/personas.md` and `docs/permissions.md`; the skill itself is ours and ships in the bundle. There is no third file — see § *Pinned*.

This task is the next increment on that fine-grained permissioning journey and we've chosen to tie the permissioning model to a cast of personas, not a list of "roles", partially to highlight the difference between our ReBAC, not RBAC, model. That case is made in [auth.md](../docs/vision/auth.md) § *The layers a call passes* — the M7 data-plane DAG bullet, and the *Why relationships rather than roles?* paragraph beneath it, which cites AuthZed on role explosion.

## Objective and goals

**Objective -- create a de✨light✨ful experience for testing a Studio authored app as each persona.**

**Goals:**

1. **Provision each persona as a user.** Every time the personas file changes, either provision from scratch, if the .dev Star was wiped, or adjust the existing provisioning to match the latest version of that file. *(Since ADR-022 nothing is provisioned in the Registry: provisioning is each persona's nodes, edges and grants, keyed on the id its host spells, plus the admin mark the dev Star keeps for the persona that holds one.)*
2. **Populate with (on a wipe), or adjust existing (no wipe), test data to minimally show off the difference in each persona's permissions.** *(In scope, as the build's last phase, so the tabs can ship first if it drags. Writing as a persona on the server, most of its machinery, is needed anyway to write each persona's name; § *Pinned* carries how.)*
3. **Log each persona in to a separate tab in the preview area.** The preview area gets a set of tabs with the persona slug as the tab label (in addition to a "Me" tab as the user-dev that will be present even before personas are defined).
4. **Every collaborator in a Galaxy can test as the cast.** A Galaxy has exactly two kinds of collaborator. A Universe and/or Galaxy admin, such as Jennifer, can do everything, including invites, the Galaxy's settings and its tenant Stars. An Austen can chat, provision the personas, open their tabs and the Me tab, and wipe the dev data, but cannot invite anyone, change the Galaxy's settings, or most importantly, reach its tenant Stars. We trust her with helping build the app but not the app's tenants' data.

## Design intent — a cast on hosts of its own

**The examples follow one app.** Jennifer, a real pre-alpha user, is an admin of the Universe `acme`. By a Galaxy admin this file means anyone with dominion over the Galaxy, which includes every admin of its Universe, so Jennifer is a Galaxy admin of the app `crm`. Austen, also real, builds `crm` with Jennifer as a collaborator and holds no admin bit at the Galaxy. The app's cast has three personas: Mary, the account's admin, and Sally and Gerry, two of her team. Mary's page is at `mary--dev.crm.acme.lumenize.dev`.

**Provisioning a cast is Galaxy work; being one is a page on the persona's own host.** In a poster's turn, the Galaxy hands the procedure to its dev Star, which builds the tree, attaches each persona's grants and seeds their data. Studio frames one host per open persona, and the page there gets a token as Mary from the cookie of the user-developer who opened it. Nothing is created in the Registry: Mary has no address, no membership, no invite to accept and no cookie of her own.

- **Every guard here answers one test: does it stop lateral movement, or a production risk?** If neither, it goes. Risk is probability times cost, and someone taking control of a persona costs next to nothing, so nothing here is built to protect personas themselves. Lateral movement is reaching another Galaxy or a sibling Star; a production risk is writing as a real person or reaching a Star with real users. Both of those are a big deal, and every guard that survives stops one of them.
- **Each persona has a host of its own, because the host is how one browser holds several identities at once.** In production every cookie in a browser belongs to one person, though perhaps to more than one of their Profiles. Personas are the one case where a browser acts as several people, and the host keeps them apart without a cookie each:
  - **The refresh reads the page's host from `Origin`, which page script cannot set.** So the host alone names the persona to mint, and nothing the page sends is an input. On the dev Star's own host the same cookie mints the opener, which is the `Me` tab.
  - **Browser state is per origin.** A Client keeps its tab id in `sessionStorage` and checks it for duplicates over `BroadcastChannel`, both per origin, so persona frames on separate hosts cannot collide the way frames sharing one origin would.
- **A persona's id is computed from its host, so nothing stores which id is whose.** Its `sub` and `profileId` are one value: a hash of `acme.crm.dev/mary`, formatted as a version-5 UUID, from `personaId` in `packages/mesh/src/auth/hosts.ts`. The id is stored wherever it is used, in a grant or a seeded row, but no table maps Mary to it; every reader recomputes it. So a grant attached before Mary's tab ever opens names the `sub` her token will carry, and a `.dev` wipe changes nothing about who she is.
- **Everyone in the chat holds admin over the dev Star, so the chat floor gates the dev workspace.** Only a Galaxy admin can invite anyone into a Galaxy, and an invite's `.dev` membership gets the admin bit whenever the inviter has dominion over the Galaxy, so every collaborator is a Galaxy admin or an Austen (goal 4). Provisioning and the wipe box on Apply therefore sit at the chat floor. That gives no one anything new: each chat member could already do both from the dev Star's own page, where their token carries the `.dev` admin bit.
- **The Galaxy owns provisioning.** It runs the procedure in the poster's turn and hands it to the dev Star, which keeps the cast's admin mark beside its grants. It hands off by an ordinary `lmz.call` on a chain it starts, and that chain acts as the admin of the Galaxy's own `.dev` Star and of nothing else (§ *Pinned*). Seeding runs on the server as each persona, so the list Sally creates is genuinely Sally's. Nothing reaps a persona's Profile: it holds only public fields that anyone can already read.
- **A persona's page gets its token from the cookie of whoever opened it.** ADR-022 allows that only in a Star with no real users, today only `.dev`, and only where the cookie could open the Star's own page. Studio frames one such page per open tab.
- **The token is plain:** a persona's `sub` and `profileId`, `acme.crm.dev` as `authScope` and `aud`, and no `act`. Mary, as the account's admin, also carries `scopeAdmin`; Sally and Gerry do not. ADR-022 trades attribution for testing fidelity here, which costs nothing on a Star with no real users.

**It took three wrong turns to get here, and each is the obvious first answer.**

1. **One server-side executor does all of it**: mint, accept each persona off inbound mail, then write that persona's data by impersonating it. A Durable Object holds `callContext.originAuth`, which is verified claims rather than a replayable credential, so it has no session to mint from. And seeding runs after the mail round trip, by which time nothing holds the authority to do it. The same fact is why the Galaxy cannot simply act as the poster on the dev Star (question 9).
2. **Tabs run under impersonation**, minted by Studio off the user-developer's own session and handed into each iframe. The parent has to hand a renewal closure across a realm boundary, which [backlog.md](backlog.md) § *Lumenize Mesh* forbids by name, and whose failure is a tab quietly renewing as the user-developer while its label still reads `mary`. A tab also outlives the user-developer's logout, and the gate meant to select a persona tab cannot tell the user-developer's own. Impersonation stays what it was built for, the admin-debug flow in [backlog.md](backlog.md) § *Nebula Studio UI*; a persona tab is not it.
3. **Every tab is a real login** (2026-09-09 to 2026-09-18): a real account per persona at `acme.crm.dev~mary@personas.lumenize.io`, a refresh cookie per membership so eight tabs at one scope stop overwriting each other, and the invite link handed to Studio instead of a mailbox. Two of its parts sat in the code that issues real memberships: a membership born accepted, an exception to [ADR-012](../docs/adr/012-global-profile-visibility.md)'s rule that authority rests on an accepted membership, and guards keeping every human out of the persona addresses. A mistake in either reaches real people, which the risk test counts as a production risk. A persona with no address needs neither.

## The order it runs in — a sketch, expected to change

⚠️ **Roughed out 2026-09-09, re-cut 2026-10-08 for ADR-022, and deliberately not pinned** (Larry: *we'll have to evolve this with trial and error*). Provisioning does one pass on the server, and a tab only frames a host.

**① In the poster's turn, on the dev Star.** Nodes and edges need no ontology, and every persona's `sub` is computable, so the tree, every grant and the seed land in one pass:

1. **node** → **edge**, building the tree
2. **grant** — `setPermission` per persona, naming `personaId(star, slug)` and a node from step 1
3. **seed**, as each persona in procedure order: its name first, then its data steps whose target node is empty
4. write the record of whom it provisioned
5. return the realized tree
